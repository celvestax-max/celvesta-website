Step-by-step guides for running your Celvesta hosted switch: logging in, connecting customers and vendors, setting up routes and rates, and reading your call reports.

Part 1

Getting started

Your Celvesta switch comes with two web panels. You manage everything in the Admin panel. Your own customers use the Customer portal to check their balance and calls.

Admin panel

For you, the switch owner. Set up customers, vendors, gateways, routes, rates and payments, and see all traffic.

Customer portal

For your customers. They see their own balance, call records, active calls and statistics. They cannot change your settings.

  1. Log in to the Admin panel

    Open the Admin panel address from your Celvesta welcome email and sign in with the username and password we sent you.

  2. Change your password

    Go to System → Admin Users, open your user, click Edit, set a new password and save. Use a long password that you don't use anywhere else.

    If other staff need access, create a separate user for each person here instead of sharing one login.

  3. Look at the Dashboard

    The Dashboard is the first page after login. It shows your current traffic and recent activity, so you can check at a glance that calls are flowing.

  4. Know your plan limits

    Each Celvesta plan runs on its own server, with a set number of concurrent calls (CC) and calls per second (CPS):

    StarterUp to 200 CC · 20 CPS
    BusinessUp to 500 CC · 50 CPS
    Carrier1,000+ CC · custom

    When traffic goes above your plan limit, the extra calls are rejected. If you are close to the limit often, contact Celvesta to move to a bigger plan. See all plans and prices.

  5. Give a customer portal access

    Go to System → Customer Portal Logins and click New.

    CustomerThe customer this login belongs to.
    AccountsWhich of their accounts they can see. Leave empty to show all of their accounts.
    Login / PasswordWhat the customer types on the portal login page.
    Allowed IPsOptional. Only these IP addresses can log in. Separate several with commas.

    Send the customer the portal address together with their login. You need to create the customer first (see Part 2).

Part 2

Connecting traffic

Every call comes in from a customer, passes through your switch and goes out to a vendor. Before you can route calls, you create both sides: the company, its billing account, and its SIP gateway. Do these steps in order.

Customer gateway→ Your Celvesta switch→ Vendor gateway

  1. Create the companies

    Go to Billing → Contractors and click New. Create one contractor for each company you work with.

    • For a company that sends you calls, tick Customer.
    • For a company you send calls to, tick Vendor.
    • Make sure Enabled is ticked. A company can be both customer and vendor.

  2. Create a billing account for each company

    Go to Billing → Accounts and click New. Choose the contractor and give the account a name. A new account always starts at a balance of zero.

    Min balanceFor customers. If the balance falls below this, their calls are blocked. Use 0 for prepaid customers.
    Max balanceFor vendors. Set it higher than the current balance, for example 100. If the balance goes above it, the vendor's routes stop being used.
    Origination capacityOptional. Most calls this customer can send at the same time.
    Termination capacityOptional. Most calls you can send to this vendor at the same time.
    Common mistake: a vendor account with Max balance left at 0. The vendor's routes will not be used and calls fail with no route.

  3. Add the customer's gateway

    Go to Equipment → Gateways and click New.

    ContractorThe customer company.
    EnabledOn.
    Allow originationOn. This gateway sends calls to you.
    Allow terminationOff.

  4. Add the vendor's gateway

    Go to Equipment → Gateways and click New again.

    ContractorThe vendor company.
    EnabledOn.
    Allow originationOff.
    Allow terminationOn. You send calls to this gateway.
    Host / PortThe vendor's SIP IP address and port, as given by your vendor.

    If your vendor gave you a tech prefix, add it in the gateway's number settings so it is put in front of every number you send.

  5. Exchange IPs with both sides

    Send your switch's SIP IP address to your customer (they send calls to it) and to your vendor (they need to whitelist it). The customer's IP address is entered in Part 3, step 6.

Part 3

Routing & rates

Routing decides which vendor gets each call and what you pay them. Rates decide what you charge your customer. Both match calls by number prefix, such as 1 for USA or 44 for the UK. The longest matching prefix wins.

  1. Create a routing group

    Under the Routing menu, open Routing Groups and create one, for example Wholesale routes. A routing group is a folder that holds your routes.

  2. Create a routing plan

    Go to Routing → Routing Plans and click New. Give it a name, choose the routing group from step 1, and pick a sorting method. LCR sends each call to the cheapest vendor first, then tries the next one if it fails.

  3. Add routes (dialpeers)

    A dialpeer is one route: “send calls starting with this prefix to this vendor, at this cost.” Go to Routing → Dialpeers and click New.

    PrefixThe numbers this route covers, for example 1 or 44. Enter several prefixes separated by commas to create one dialpeer for each.
    Routing groupThe group from step 1.
    Vendor / Account / GatewayThe vendor, their account and their gateway from Part 2.
    Initial rateYour cost per minute for the first billing interval.
    Next rateYour cost per minute after that.

    Add a dialpeer from a second vendor on the same prefix to get a backup route.

  4. Create a rateplan

    Create a Rate Group first, then go to Routing → Rateplans, click New, name it (for example Standard) and add the rate group. Use different rateplans when different customers pay different prices.

  5. Add your selling prices (destinations)

    Go to Routing → Destinations and click New. Enter the prefix, choose the rate group, and set the Initial rate and Next rate you charge per minute. Celvesta bills to 4 decimal places.

    Example. You pay your vendor $0.0120 for the first minute and $0.0100 per minute after. You charge your customer $0.0180 and $0.0150. For a 10-minute call:
    Customer pays: 0.0180 + 9 × 0.0150 = $0.1530
    You pay vendor: 0.0120 + 9 × 0.0100 = $0.1020
    Your profit: $0.0510
    On 1,000,000 minutes a month, a margin like this is about $5,100 profit.

  6. Link everything with Customers Auth

    Customers Auth is how the switch recognises a customer's call by its IP address. Go to Routing → Customers Auths and click New.

    Customer / Account / GatewayThe customer, their account and their gateway from Part 2.
    RateplanThe rateplan from step 4.
    Routing planThe routing plan from step 2.
    IPThe customer's IP address the calls come from.
    Calls rejected with disconnect code 110? The switch could not match the call to a customer. Check the IP address in Customers Auth, and that the customer and account are enabled.

  7. Add balance to the customer

    Go to Billing → Payments, click New, choose the customer's account and enter the amount. The balance must be above the account's Min balance, or calls are blocked.

  8. Test before going live

    Go to Routing → Routing Simulation. Enter the customer's IP address, a port (for example 5060), a caller number and a destination number, then click Simulate routing. The result shows which route and price would be used, with a full log underneath. No real call is made.

Part 4

Reports & CDRs

Every call creates a call detail record (CDR) with its time, numbers, duration, price and result. Use these pages to check quality, solve disputes and bill your customers.

Call records

CDR → CDR History

Search calls by date, customer, vendor or number. Open a call to see why it ended.

Live calls

Realtime Data → Active Calls

See calls in progress right now and compare them with your plan's CC limit.

Customer traffic

Reports → Customer Traffic

Minutes, revenue, ASR and ACD for each customer over a time range.

Vendor traffic

Reports → Vendor Traffic

Minutes, cost, ASR and ACD for each vendor. Use it to spot bad routes.

Export CDRs

CDR → CDR Export

Create a file of call records for a customer or date range to share or reconcile.

Unknown IPs

Reports → Not authenticated attempts

Calls from IPs that match no customer. Helps find setup mistakes and scanners.

What your customers see

In the Customer portal, your customers can view their balance, their own CDRs, active calls and call statistics, plus the IP addresses authorised for their account. They only ever see their own data.

Reading quality numbers

  • ASR going down on one vendor usually means that route is failing. Move traffic to a backup dialpeer.
  • ACD that is very short (a few seconds) can mean poor audio quality or false answers.
  • Check the disconnect code on failed calls in CDR History. It tells you whether the call failed at your switch, at the vendor, or because of balance or capacity.
Reference

Glossary

CC
Concurrent calls. How many calls are up at the same moment.
CPS
Calls per second. How many new calls can start each second.
ASR
Answer-seizure ratio. The percentage of calls that are answered.
ACD
Average call duration of answered calls, in minutes.
CDR
Call detail record. One line of data for one call.
Prefix
The first digits of a phone number, used to pick a route and a rate.
Dialpeer
One route to one vendor for one prefix, with your cost.
Destination
One selling price to your customer for one prefix.
LCR
Least-cost routing. Try the cheapest vendor first.

Contact Celvesta support and include the time of the call, the numbers dialled and any disconnect code you see.
